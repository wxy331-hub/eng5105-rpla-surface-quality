# SOTA and Evidence-Gap Map: Laboratory-Scale rPLA Filament Surface-Quality Monitoring

## Scope and evidence boundary

This is a research map, not report prose. It is limited to six source clusters and uses peer-reviewed original research, standards, and official manufacturer or regulator documentation. The local project brief requires a laboratory-scale, real-time surface-quality monitoring system suitable for later industrial integration; it identifies bubbles, roughness, contamination, cracks, and inclusions as candidate surface defects and explicitly names lighting control, image processing, and defect classification as design challenges (Project Brief, pp. 7-8).

Local records currently establish only `LIVE_INTEGRATION_ONLY` or `OFFLINE_SYNTHETIC_ONLY` evidence. They do not establish real-rPLA defect accuracy, calibrated physical size or position, an achieved 20 processed-frame/s target, or industrial line-speed capability. Literature and manufacturer data below therefore constrain the proposal and validation plan; they do not upgrade the project's evidence status.

## Six-cluster SOTA and gap map

| Cluster / candidate contribution | What primary or authoritative sources establish | Local user evidence | Real gap for this project | Claim strength | Main risk / forbidden inference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. rPLA/FFF feedstock quality and visible surface condition** | Original experiments show that rPLA processing conditions and recycling history can affect extrudability, filament/part surface condition, dimensional behavior, rheology, and mechanical performance. For example, recycled PLA required different filament-extrusion conditions and printed parts had lower strength than virgin controls in one 2024 study [S1]. PLA filament-condition experiments associate bubbles and other defects with poor repeatability, surface quality, and tensile behavior [S2]. Closed-loop recycling experiments found molecular/viscosity degradation and surface pinholes after repeated cycles [S13]. | The project brief selects filament **surface quality** and lists bubbles, roughness, contamination, cracks, and inclusions. No labelled real-rPLA samples have yet been inspected. | There is no demonstrated mapping from this project's camera-visible candidate regions to material defect classes, process causes, customer acceptance, or mechanical consequences. A labelled defect taxonomy and physical reference method are still needed. | **Moderate** for the general relevance of surface condition to quality; **none yet** for this detector's classes or thresholds. | Do not claim that every bright/dark region is a defect, that appearance reveals root cause, or that printed-part studies directly validate as-extruded filament inspection. |
| **2. Machine-vision inspection and controlled illumination** | A peer-reviewed surface-inspection system using photometric stereo demonstrated that controlled multi-light imaging can locate suspect regions before higher-resolution measurement [S3]. This supports illumination as part of the measurement system, especially where shape, highlights, and shadows influence contrast; it does not prescribe one universal lighting geometry. | Plan B includes controlled lighting, a fixed ROI, candidate overlays, and OpenCV processing. Current live images include overexposure/scene structure and are explicitly not rPLA-defect evidence. | The actual rPLA fixture still needs fixed camera distance, focus, background, exposure, light geometry, glare control, and reference specimens. Illumination repeatability must be tested across colour, gloss, curvature, and spool/batch variation. | **Moderate** for controlled-light machine vision as an appropriate approach; **pending experimental evidence** for the selected geometry. | Do not infer that ordinary room light, one threshold, or a successful image stream produces measurement-grade inspection. Photometric-stereo results on planar steel are transferable design evidence, not direct rPLA performance evidence. |
| **3. OV3660 rolling shutter, JPEG/MJPEG, buffering, and Wi-Fi constraints** | The OV3660 datasheet specifies a rolling shutter, compressed output, 2048 x 1536 active array, and sensor-level maximum transfer rates that depend on resolution [S4]. Espressif's official camera driver warns that RGB/YUV can lose image data under PSRAM/Wi-Fi load, recommends JPEG capture for performance, and documents the control/throughput trade-off between one and multiple frame buffers [S5]. These are component/driver ceilings, not end-to-end guarantees. | Live hardware records show 26.35 FPS at 640 x 480 in an early 30 s stream test, but the selected SVGA path produced 10.77 FPS in a 30 s throughput test and only 13.96-15.60 FPS in shorter windows. A brightness-step test measured 101.32 ms median and 222.99 ms P95 from control request through physical camera, Wi-Fi MJPEG, Python processing, and API JPEG publication; it lacks exposure and browser-paint timestamps. | Motion distortion, exposure/lighting interaction, JPEG artefacts, radio contention, decode load, buffering policy, and resident-service contention must be isolated experimentally at fixed SVGA. The proposed 20 processed-frame/s target remains an unresolved design gap. | **Strong** for the existence of rolling-shutter and resource/transport constraints; **project-measured** for the stated local chain results; **unknown** for causal attribution or final throughput. | Do not quote sensor maximum FPS as ESP32S3, Wi-Fi, OpenCV, GUI, or line-speed performance. Do not name rolling shutter, Wi-Fi, or JPEG as the measured bottleneck without an isolation test. |
| **4. ESP32S3/OV3660, Raspberry Pi 4, and STM32 platform roles** | Seeed's official documentation confirms the XIAO ESP32S3 Sense variant with OV3660, dual-core ESP32-S3 up to 240 MHz, 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, 8 MB PSRAM/flash, camera connection, and 2048 x 1536 sensor support [S6]. Raspberry Pi's product brief confirms a quad-core 64-bit Cortex-A72 SoC, 1-8 GB RAM, Ethernet/Wi-Fi, USB, GPIO, and Linux-capable storage/interfaces [S7]. ST's current Nucleo-32 brief confirms the NUCLEO-L432KC/STM32L432KCU6, ST-LINK, Virtual COM port, and prototype-oriented expansion environment [S8]. | The adopted architecture assigns acquisition/streaming to ESP32S3/OV3660, resident OpenCV/API/SQLite/GUI work to Raspberry Pi 4, and encoder/watchdog/lighting/safe-I/O work to STM32. The current resident service is validated on Windows, not yet on the Pi/STM32 hardware chain. | Platform capability is plausible, but deployment throughput, thermals, power integrity, serial timing, encoder calibration, watchdog timing, and safe-state behavior remain unverified on final hardware. | **Strong** for documented component capabilities; **proposal-level** for role allocation; **none yet** for final-system timing or reliability. | A CPU, RAM, interface, or board feature is not an application benchmark. Do not claim deterministic safety, Pi FPS, STM32 response time, or industrial suitability from datasheets alone. |
| **5. Validation metrics and independent batch/spool/event testing** | For imbalanced binary tasks, precision-recall analysis can be more informative than ROC analysis [S9]. Small-sample cross-validation can have large and underestimated uncertainty, so point estimates alone can overstate evidence [S10]. These sources support reporting a confusion matrix, per-class precision/recall, F1, false-alarm and miss counts/rates, sample counts, confidence/uncertainty, and clearly held-out testing. | Current records contain no real-rPLA labels, confusion matrix, precision, recall, F1, minimum detectable defect, or independent test set. The local plan already proposes collection by batch or spool and multi-frame event tracking. | Define the unit of independence before data collection. Separate training/tuning and final testing by physical spool, material batch, filament segment, or defect event so adjacent frames of the same event do not appear on both sides. Report both frame-level and event-level results if both are used. | **Strong** for metric choice and caution about small samples; **methodologically justified project assumption** for spool/batch/event grouping until a domain-specific standard is identified. | Do not randomly split adjacent video frames and present the result as independent generalisation. Do not report accuracy alone, hide class prevalence, tune on the final test set, or treat fold-to-fold spread as the full uncertainty. |
| **6. Privacy, machinery safety, and sustainability governance** | OAIC guidance states that identifiable images can be personal information and that workplace monitoring must comply with relevant Australian and state/territory laws [S11]. ISO 13849-1:2023 provides a methodology for safety-related parts of machinery control systems but does not choose the safety function or required performance level for this application [S12]. Closed-loop PLA experiments/LCA found potential environmental advantages over landfill/incineration under the studied boundary, while also finding material degradation [S13]; ISO 14040 requires explicit LCA goal, scope, inventory, impact assessment, interpretation, limitations, reporting, and review [S14]. | The intended camera is fixture-facing; Plan B proposes local processing, an STM32 heartbeat/watchdog, lighting control, and a safe actuator/marker interface. No safety performance level, actuator wiring validation, privacy assessment, energy measurement, or project-specific LCA exists. | Crop/fixture-mask images and minimize retention by design; document access/export/deletion. Perform machinery risk assessment and define safe states before enabling outputs. Treat sustainability as a testable lifecycle claim including added electronics, energy, false rejects, and avoided waste. | **Strong** for governance frameworks; **pending** for compliance and net environmental benefit in this installation. | A local database is not automatically privacy compliant; a heartbeat is not a certified safety function; use of recycled material or low-cost electronics does not by itself prove sustainability. |

## Project-specific assumptions that must remain labelled

| Assumption | Why it is reasonable | Evidence needed before promotion to a result |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed camera, focus, background, ROI, and controlled lighting will make candidate detection sufficiently repeatable. | Consistent acquisition is foundational to machine vision, and controlled-light methods can enhance defect contrast [S3]. | Repeated reference-specimen trials across sessions, operators, spools/batches, colours/gloss levels, line positions, and representative speeds. |
| Spool-, batch-, segment-, or event-separated evaluation will better estimate generalisation than a random frame split. | Adjacent frames and repeated views of one physical event are correlated; independent held-out testing and small-sample caution are supported by [S9-S10]. | Pre-registered grouping rule, immutable held-out set, per-group counts, and sensitivity analysis across grouping choices. |
| Raspberry Pi 4 can host the resident vision/data/UI service while STM32 handles timing-critical I/O and safe-state coordination. | Manufacturer interfaces and processing resources support feasibility [S7-S8]. | On-target deployment benchmark, CPU/RAM/thermal/power logs, serial timing tests, encoder calibration, fault injection, and wiring-level safe-state validation. |
| Local fixture-facing acquisition can reduce privacy exposure. | Data minimisation and avoidance of identifiable images reduce the conditions that trigger image-privacy concerns [S11]. | Verified field of view, masking/cropping, access and retention policy, deployment-specific legal/privacy review, and operator notice where applicable. |
| Detecting surface anomalies will reduce waste and improve sustainability. | Earlier quality feedback could prevent downstream waste, while rPLA recycling can have benefits under defined boundaries [S13-S14]. | Baseline-versus-prototype inventory including energy, electronics, false rejects, avoided scrap/rework, maintenance, and end-of-life; no net-benefit claim before comparison. |

## Gap summary

1. **Most defensible technical gap:** a low-cost laboratory system that links controlled imaging of moving rPLA filament to traceable candidate events and then validates those events on physically independent spools/batches. The novelty is the bounded evidence chain and validation discipline, not an unproven claim of industrial-grade classification.
2. **Most defensible systems gap:** a fail-aware Plan B demonstrator that explicitly measures the OV3660/Wi-Fi/MJPEG/Pi processing envelope and inhibits detection or output during unhealthy acquisition states. The present architecture is plausible, but final SVGA throughput, motion-image quality, calibration, and safe I/O remain test obligations.

## Search and verification note

- Search date: 15 August 2026.
- Included: peer-reviewed original experiments directly relevant to rPLA/PLA quality, machine-vision surface inspection, evaluation methodology, and lifecycle effects; official current manufacturer documents; current ISO records; Australian privacy regulator guidance.
- Excluded: vendor blogs, tertiary summaries, preprints where a peer-reviewed or official source was available, unverified cost/performance claims, and studies whose reported accuracy could not be transferred to moving rPLA filament.
- Known coverage limitation: no directly comparable peer-reviewed study was identified that validates an ESP32S3/OV3660 Wi-Fi MJPEG system for moving rPLA-filament surface-defect classification. That absence is treated as a project gap, not proof of novelty.
