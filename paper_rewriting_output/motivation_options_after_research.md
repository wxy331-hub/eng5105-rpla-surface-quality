# Motivation Options After Research

| Option | One-Sentence Motivation | Core Innovation | Why It Is Not Overbroad | Required Evidence | Best-Fit Report Arc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Feasibility-led, evidence-traceable design (recommended)** | BioLoop needs a surface-quality monitor that can be delivered from the available university hardware within the semester, so the proposal develops Plan B as a traceable acquisition-to-decision system and subjects every unverified performance claim to staged validation after material arrival. | A feasibility gate followed by a requirement-to-architecture-to-verification chain: Plan A remains a technically credible comparator but is eliminated by unavailable hardware, excessive Plan-A-specific incremental cost, procurement lead time, and delivery risk. | It claims a rigorous and implementable proposal, not industrial-grade detection or novel computer vision. Existing live/offline results support integration readiness only. | Official hardware availability; current Plan-A-specific supplier prices/lead times; Plan B architecture; current integration tests; stakeholder consultation record when available; Week 5+ optical/encoder calibration and labelled real-rPLA testing. | Client problem -> literature/stakeholder needs -> proposed requirements -> feasibility gates -> Plan A/B trade-off -> Plan B functional design -> bounded current evidence -> post-arrival validation, risks, BOM, schedule -> recommendation. |
| **B — Fail-aware cyber-physical inspection chain** | A low-cost camera is useful only when stale, disconnected, or uncalibrated data cannot silently become a quality-control action, so the proposal centres Plan B on health gating, evidence status, local records, and STM32-mediated safe outputs. | Integrates latest-frame buffering, acquisition health states, explicit evidence labels, watchdog coordination, and safe-action inhibition into the sensor-to-API-to-actuator chain. | It does not call the watchdog a certified safety function and does not claim physical defect performance; it proposes safety-oriented behaviour that still requires final wiring and risk validation. | Current fault-injection/recovery results; formal serial and heartbeat contracts; power and interface calculations; final Pi/STM32 deployment tests; machinery risk assessment and safe-state verification. | Failure consequence -> requirements -> two concepts -> Plan B architecture/state model -> communications/API/power -> fault evidence -> governance and residual risk -> implementation and verification plan. |
| **C — Validation-first rPLA surface measurement** | Because ordinary video anomalies cannot yet be treated as rPLA defects, the proposal defines a controlled-imaging and validation method that converts Plan B candidate events into defensible defect classes, calibrated sizes, positions, and operating limits only after independent real-material testing. | An evidence ladder from controlled optics and pixel candidates to physical calibration, spool/batch-separated ground truth, event-level metrics, and bounded operating envelopes. | It does not promise a minimum defect size, 90% performance, 20 FPS, or industrial line speed before testing; the contribution is the validation design rather than an achieved detector. | Fixed fixture and illumination; reference defects; pixels/mm and encoder calibration; independent spool/batch/event split; confusion matrix, precision/recall/F1, false alarms per metre, latency, stability, and speed sweeps after Week 5 Thursday. | Quality problem -> limitations of visual inference -> literature and stakeholder criteria -> concept comparison -> selected optical/software chain -> calibration and experimental design -> acceptance gates -> risks, timeline, and recommendation. |

## Synthesis note

All three options satisfy the scene analysis, use the exemplar pattern of a single traceable decision spine, and occupy gaps identified by the SOTA map. Option A offers the broadest rubric coverage without overclaiming and can absorb the strongest elements of Options B and C as supporting design principles rather than competing motivations.
