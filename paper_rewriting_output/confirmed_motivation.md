# Confirmed Motivation

## Exact confirmed motivation

BioLoop requires an rPLA surface-quality monitoring concept that can be delivered from the available university hardware within the semester. The report will therefore use a feasibility-led, evidence-traceable argument: Plan A remains a technically credible comparator but is eliminated by unavailable university hardware, excessive Plan-A-specific incremental cost, procurement lead-time risk, and semester deliverability; Plan B alone proceeds to implementation through a traceable chain from proposed stakeholder requirements to system architecture, bounded integration evidence, fault-safe behaviour, and staged real-material validation.

## Supporting design principles

- **Safety principle derived from Option B:** stale, disconnected, unhealthy, or uncalibrated data must not silently become a quality decision or automatic output. Acquisition health gating, latest-frame handling, STM32 watchdog coordination, and safe-output inhibition are integral Plan B functions.
- **Validation principle derived from Option C:** candidate image regions become defensible rPLA defect classes, physical sizes, positions, and operating limits only after controlled optical calibration and independent real-material testing. Until then, performance claims remain explicitly bounded.

## Rejected alternatives

- Option B was not selected as the whole-report motivation because a safety-only structure would under-emphasise the rubric's required client need, concept comparison, procurement, BOM, and project-delivery argument. Its strongest mechanisms remain in Plan B.
- Option C was not selected as the whole-report motivation because the deliverable is a proposal rather than a completed validation study. Its evidence ladder remains the governing validation strategy.

## Scope limits and forbidden overclaims

- Do not claim that Plan B has achieved real-rPLA defect accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, minimum detectable physical defect size, millimetre position accuracy, final usable line speed, or industrial readiness.
- Do not treat live streaming, brightness-step latency, fault recovery, synthetic candidates, or database/UI operation as real-material performance evidence.
- Do not state that stakeholders approved numerical requirements unless a consultation record is supplied.
- Do not invent supplier quotations, stock status, procurement lead times, measured costs, consultation dates, participant names, calibration data, power measurements, or safety certification.
- Compare Plan A cost using only Plan-A-specific incremental items. Shared Raspberry Pi, STM32, lighting, mechanical, and auxiliary components must not be double-counted.
- Plan B is the sole implementation and validation path; the report must not imply a later implementation or migration to Plan A.
